HOW THE ESTIMATE WORKS

Brisket flat, brisket point, packer brisket, pork shoulder, baby back ribs, spare ribs, poultry, fish, and lamb do not cook on the same clock. This BBQ smoke time calculator uses cut-specific profiles. For large roasts, weight helps. For ribs, fillets, and portions, thickness and shape usually matter more.

A stall can delay brisket or pulled pork by hours. Wrapping, bone, fat, weather, and smoker behavior also change the finish time. Use the displayed window to plan your cook. Allow extra time for big cuts and check internal temperature or tenderness before serving.

For food safety, whole-cut beef, pork, and lamb need at least 145°F followed by a three-minute rest. Poultry needs 165°F, and fish needs 145°F. BBQ tenderness targets for brisket, ribs, and pulled pork are often much higher. See the FoodSafety.gov chart.
